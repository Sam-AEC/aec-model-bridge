The Span

One mark, sixteen colors, one icon grammar.

A span, drawn flat. The two nodes are the model and the agent; the apex above them is this product. Deck and nodes are drawn in ink because they already exist — the apex is the only thing painted in brand color, because it is the only thing the bridge adds.

Nothing draws a line from the nodes up to the apex. They are tied together by alignment: the apex base and the deck are both exactly 0.340 wide and sit on the same two axes. Connector lines were drawn five different ways and every one of them turned the mark into a capital A.

It is the Connect icon already shipping in the ribbon, reproportioned and promoted. Circles and straight lines only, which is what lets SVG, WPF and CSS each reproduce it exactly — something the three different shaded cubes it replaces never managed.

The sizes it ships at

16 and 32 px on the Revit ribbon, 26 px in the panel nav rail, ~300 px in the README lockup.

One geometry at every size. Nothing is dropped or re-tuned for small sizes, so there is no optical-size variant to keep in sync across three renderers by hand.

Only the stroke changes: size × 0.06, snapped to half a pixel, floor 1.5 px — the arithmetic IconGenerator.StrokeWidth already does.

Why nothing connects the apex

The brief's motif has lines running from each node up to the apex. Drawn literally, they extend the apex's own legs and the deck becomes a crossbar.

Five variants, all the same outcome. The alignment of the apex base to the deck says “these belong together” without spelling the letter.

The panel

Same markup the panel ships today — 58 px rail, 64 px topbar, card list — repainted with these tokens and the mark dropped into the rail in place of the gradient square.

The rail stays dark in both themes. It is the product's spine, and it is the one place the brand indicator lives.

Rail and topbar are the only translucent surfaces. Cards stay opaque — glass behind body text is a legibility tax.

Severity

One set of six, shared by the ribbon icons and the panel badges. Solid fill on light, 14 % tint with a hairline on dark.

Every light fill clears 4.5:1 against its own white label. The amber shipping today does not — it measures 3.6:1.

Sentence case, not caps. Uppercase at 11 px costs legibility on a word like “warning” and the pill shape already says “status”.

Icon grammar

Structure is ink. One accent, and only if the icon reports a state.

Five state icons carry a semantic colour. Four action icons carry none — ink strokes, ink-tinted interiors. That is what makes a ribbon scannable instead of eight unrelated stickers.

Brand cyan appears on the mark and nowhere else in the ribbon, which is what makes the brand button findable.

Sixteen tokens

Down from the twenty-two unrelated hexes spread across logo.svg, IconGenerator.cs and styles.css today.

Brand cyan is the one saturated family no semantic token occupies, so the brand can never be misread as a status. It is deliberately not Autodesk's #0696D7 — the panel is a guest inside Revit, not a part of it.

Type

Segoe UI stays, for everything, and this is now a decision rather than an accident.

The panel is a docked pane inside Revit. A webfont would flash on every open and would make the product look like a web page embedded in Revit instead of a part of it. The distinctiveness budget is spent on the mark and the icon grammar.
